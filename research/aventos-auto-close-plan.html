Aventos Auto-Close Plan

An ESP32 box that notices when the Blum AVENTOS flap has been opened and sends the SERVO-DRIVE a close command five minutes later. This page summarises what the research established, recommends an architecture, stages the work with go/no-go gates, and lists the decisions and home checks needed before anything is bought or built.

Your system: AVENTOS HF top. That is the top-generation drive unit 23.A000 (set) / 23.A001 (unit), FCC ID W95-23A001, sold in the EU since 2023, with the buttons Link · Ok · Play · Collision · Reset. Switches are linked by pressing Link, then each switch, then Ok. Two drives are synchronised with Link on both, Ok, then Play for a reference run. This is precisely the generation the open-source library has not been tested on, so gate G1 carries real risk and the spare-switch fallback should be ordered together with the spike parts (decision D10).

Is it Bluetooth? No evidence for it on the switch-to-drive link. The "simple bluetooth connection" wording comes from one US retailer's product bullet, not from Blum. Blum's own documents give the 23.A00x drive the same radio data as the switch (2.404–2.452 GHz, +4 dBm, "bidirectional, coded telegrams"), declare it under EN 300 440 rather than EN 300 328, the standard Bluetooth devices are tested to, and that band does not cover the full Bluetooth band of 2402–2480 MHz that a Bluetooth radio must use. The FCC filing for the drive is a Part 15.249 low-power transmitter at 2404–2452 MHz, and the switch shared by all drives is filed as "2.4 GHz proprietary, GFSK". A Bluetooth interface for a future app could still sit inside the newest drives undocumented; the XIAO ESP32-C6's own Bluetooth can scan for advertisements in Stage 1 at zero cost, which settles it either way.

Recommendation

Build a hybrid, staged device: a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6 with a genuine Nordic nRF24L01+ module that joins the drive as a virtual synchronised drive over Blum's own 2.4 GHz link, plus a reed sensor on the flap as ground truth and safety interlock. The radio path sends an absolute close command (idempotent, no toggle risk) and receives the drive's move events. The reed sensor starts the timer for every kind of opening, including a press on the front, which produces no radio frame from the switch at all. If the radio path fails its gate on your drive generation, the same box drives a spare Blum switch through an isolated PhotoMOS contact instead, which is the proven fallback.

Why not "catch the switch's OPEN frame" alone? The Blum switch sends only a trigger; the drive decides open or close from its own position. And the normal way to open an AVENTOS flap is a push on the front, which never involves the switch. A device that only listens to the switch would miss most openings and could not tell open from close. The sensor plus the drive's own move events fix both problems.

What the research established

Status chips: confirmed quoted from a primary source and checked by an independent verifier · uncertain plausible, not yet proven · corrected partly wrong in first research, fixed · inferred engineering inference, no source.

  • confirmed
    Every current SERVO-DRIVE radio part (switch 23P5020, drives 21FA00x, 23.A00x, 23KA00x, flex receiver) is specified at 2.404–2.452 GHz, at most +4 dBm (2.5 mW), about 10 m range, "bidirectional, coded telegrams". Blum declares them under EN 300 440, never a sub-GHz standard.Blum instruction leaflet BA-139 v7 (EN and DE), EU declarations of conformity 2023–2026.
  • confirmed
    The link is Nordic Enhanced ShockBurst as used by the nRF24L01+: GFSK at 1 Mbps, fixed channel 4 (2404 MHz) for operation and channel 10 (2410 MHz) for sync pairing, 5-byte addresses built as 0xDD + 3-byte device ID reversed + pipe byte, dynamic payloads, auto-ACK with ACK payloads, CRC-16. Two independent sources agree at frame level. The RF chip itself has never been photographed. The ESP32's own Wi-Fi/BLE radio cannot speak it, so an external nRF24L01+ (or an nRF52840) is required.Port-Net library source, bluhm-de protocol page, Nordic nRF24L01+ product specification, Blum patents (2008 block diagram labels the radio "nRF"; the 2020 family names ShockBurst/ESB).
  • confirmed
    An open-source ESP32 + nRF24L01+ library exists: Port-Net/BlumServoDrive. It pairs through the drive's own SYNC procedure as a virtual synchronised drive, learns the drive ID, sends absolute open/close (0x25 wake, then 0x26 move), and fires OPEN/CLOSE callbacks on drive move frames. Caveats found by reading the code: single author, 15 commits, last one 2025-01-10, no documented test hardware or drive generation; the open/close byte (0x01/0x10) is mapped inconsistently between send and receive paths; four small bugs; LICENSE says GPL-2.0 while its metadata says LGPL-3.0.github.com/Port-Net/BlumServoDrive (every file read); verifier re-checked against the live repository.
  • uncertain
    Whether a sync partner is told about every movement, including openings caused by a front press, is an untested assumption. Blum only documents that synchronised drives "move simultaneously". The library's receive path handles such frames, but nobody has logged them. This is the single most important thing Stage 1 must measure.Verifier verdict on claim C04; Blum BA-139 v7.
  • confirmed
    The radio switch sends a trigger only: a fixed command byte, its own ID and a counter, with no open/close field. The drive chooses the direction from its own position, so any switch-style actuator is a toggle. On the analysed generation the drive does not check the counter, so there is no rolling code.Blum patent DE202009010203U1, bluhm-de frame decode, Port-Net code.
  • confirmed
    A brief push on the front opens the flap (about 2 mm of distance-bumper travel). The flap can be moved by hand at any time. Neither action produces a switch frame.Blum BA-139 v7; Blum brochures 2010/2016.
  • confirmed
    No AVENTOS drive unit has a wired control input. Its only cable is the two-core 24 V DC SELV power lead. Blum forbids connecting any other device to its power supply, so the ESP32 gets its own USB-C supply.Blum BA-139 v7, MA-502, power-supply leaflets; verifier found no contradiction.
  • corrected
    Up to six switches can be paired to one drive unit, documented for both the classic drive and the HK top drive (the first research pass wrongly said "no limit documented for top"). Each switch binds to one drive. A spare 23P5020 costs about €29–38 in DE/AT. "Reset Wireless" or "reset" erases all switches and the sync and collision settings, so re-pairing must be documented.Blum Inc. AVENTOS HK top technical document 2021; Blum 2014 SERVO-DRIVE manual; lignoshop price page.
  • uncertain
    Inside the switch: a 3.0 V CR2032, 10 mA rated, PCB 23P502L00.01. The pad names "+3 V, GND, button to MCU, button to Vcc (maybe 1.8 V)" are a blogger's annotations after multimeter probing, not Blum markings; the 1.8 V figure is his guess. What is proven: feeding 3.3 V and bridging the two button pads with a floating contact for 0.8 s opens or closes an HK top flap. Blum's lean protection ignores presses held too long.daumemo build log (2026-02); Blum BA-139 v7 technical data; verifier on claim C11.
  • confirmed
    Collision detection stops opening and closing when the front meets a body part or object, even just after a close command. After a power interruption the flap stays put and the next switch press re-activates the drive. Duty rating S3 5 %, at most 10 movements per 10 minutes. Feedback is by LEDs only, there is no beeper.Blum BA-139 v7; Blum Inc. 2014 and Blum AU brochures.
  • confirmed
    Blum offers no timer, auto-close, app or API for SERVO-DRIVE for AVENTOS. The only timed close in the family is the 2.5/4/8 s hold-open of SERVO-DRIVE flex for fridge and dishwasher doors. "SERVO-DRIVE smart" is an unreleased concept.All Blum manuals opened; Blum press page; verifier on claim C14.
  • confirmed
    Three current drive families: 21FA00x for classic HF/HS/HL (buttons SWITCH / SYNC / COLL / Reset Motion / Reset Wireless), 23KA00x for HK top and 23.A00x for HF/HS/HL top (EU 2023; buttons Connect / OK / Play "test 2x"). The drive label on the left lift mechanism shows article number, BAU serial and FCC ID. Blum updated the SERVO-DRIVE electronics in September 2025, and its 2020 patent family describes optional encryption and time windows. The library's pairing flow matches the classic SYNC buttons; the top generation is untested.Blum programme pages, FCC supplier declaration, patents US12698670B2 family, Port-Net code.
  • confirmed
    No Blum document describes Bluetooth anywhere in SERVO-DRIVE for AVENTOS. The HF/HS/HL top drive 23.A00x carries the same radio data as the switch and is declared under EN 300 440 (2023 DoC), its FCC grant is a Part 15.249 transmitter at 2404–2452 MHz, and the switch is filed as "2.4 GHz proprietary, GFSK". The only "Bluetooth" mention found is a US retailer's product bullet.Blum BA-139 v7 technical data; EU DoC 23.A000/23.A001 (2023-09-06); FCC W95-23A001 and W95-23P502L00-01 records; retailer listing (McFadden's).
  • uncertain
    nRF24L01+ clones (Si24R1, BK2425/RFM7x, XN297) are reported to fail exactly the ACK-payload and dynamic-payload exchange this protocol needs. Community reports, no vendor erratum. Buy modules sold as genuine Nordic parts and test two sources before committing to a PCB.RF24 library issues and community reports.
  • confirmed
    The library's example pins match a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6 exactly (CSN D0, CE D1, SPI on D8/D9/D10, BOOT as pairing button). A XIAO ESP32-C3 also works but its MISO is GPIO9, a strapping pin, so CSN needs a pull-up or the pins must be remapped. Needs arduino-esp32 core 3.x; RF24 1.6.x masks the IRQ pin by default.Port-Net example, Seeed XIAO wikis, RF24 changelog.
  • confirmed
    Transmit limits: EU 10 mW e.i.r.p. in 2400–2483.5 MHz (ERC/REC 70-03 band i, EN 300 440); US FCC 15.249. A plain nRF24L01+ with PCB antenna at low or high power level is far below the EU limit. PA+LNA modules are unnecessary and would exceed the limit at full power.ERC/REC 70-03 (Oct 2026 edition), 47 CFR 15.249, Nordic product specification.
  • inferred
    Wi-Fi channel 1 overlaps 2404 and 2410 MHz; an ESP32 transmitting a few centimetres from the nRF24 antenna can mask a drive frame. The device should run standalone first; if Wi-Fi is added, put the router on channel 6 or higher and separate the two antennas in the enclosure.
  • confirmed
    Prior art: one implemented modified-switch build with a reed sensor (daumemo, Z-Wave, HK top, Feb 2026), one protocol write-up with paywalled addresses (bluhm-de, 2023), one unbuilt Shelly Uni plan (Jan 2025). Nothing exists in ESPHome, Home Assistant core, rtl_433, Flipper or URH.Searches in EN/DE/FR/NL/IT, GitHub code search, forums; exact negative results recorded in the raw findings.
  • confirmed
    Enclosure: YAPP_Box (MIT) is a parametric OpenSCAD project-box generator with standoffs, insert sockets, snap joins, light tubes, push buttons and mounting tabs; NopSCADlib has a XIAO fit-check model. PLA/PETG walls lose under 0.01 dB at 2.4 GHz. Keep the antenna at least 15 mm from metal and the box at least 30 mm from the Blum power supply; keep cables away from the lift's moving parts.YAPP_Box and NopSCADlib repositories, Espressif antenna guidance, Blum installation constraints.

How it would work

Components

  • Controller: Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6 (21 × 17.8 mm, USB-C, matches the library's pin map). Firmware in Arduino/PlatformIO on arduino-esp32 3.x with a patched fork of the library.
  • Radio: genuine Nordic nRF24L01+ with PCB antenna, 10 µF + 100 nF at the module pins, powered from the XIAO 3V3 rail (about 13 mA in continuous receive).
  • Flap sensor: reed switch on the carcass near the bottom front edge, magnet flush in the flap's inner face (the daumemo placement). Wired so a broken cable reads "not closed, do nothing".
  • User interface: piezo buzzer and LED for the 5 s pre-close warning, one tactile button for cancel/postpone and (long press) pairing.
  • Fallback output header: PhotoMOS AQY212EH with 330 Ω from a boot-quiet GPIO, across the button pads of a spare 23P5020 that stays on its own CR2032.
  • Power: any 5 V USB-C adapter; nothing taps the Blum 24 V supply.

Staged plan and gates

  1. Stage 0 · Identify and observe (at home, about one hour)

    Work through the home checks: label photos, the four quick tests, reed placement, power socket. Answer the decisions. Nothing is bought yet.

    Gate G0: drive generation known (classic 21FA / HK top 23KA / HF-HS-HL top 23.A), switch part number known, decisions D1–D9 answered.

  2. Stage 1 · Radio feasibility spike (one or two evenings, about €20)

    Breadboard a XIAO ESP32-C6 with a genuine nRF24L01+ and a 10 µF capacitor. Fork the library, fix its four known bugs, enable debug logging. On your HF top drive: press Link on the drive (that mode accepts switches and other drives), start the library's sync routine within its 20 s window and watch for the drive's acknowledgement on channel 10; if the drive then expects the reference run, press Play and log everything it sends. A Reset on the drive restores factory settings and only costs re-linking your switches (Link, press each switch, Ok). Also run a Bluetooth scan from the ESP32-C6 while the drive is in Link mode, to close the Bluetooth question. Then log raw frames while: pressing the switch to close, pressing it to open, pushing the front, moving the flap by hand. Finally send one absolute close with the flap open, and one with it already closed.

    Gate G1 (go/no-go): pairing succeeds; a drive-originated move frame is seen for switch-triggered moves; the absolute close works and is harmless on an already-closed flap. Bonus: front-press moves are relayed. Go → radio actuator. No-go → fallback: link the spare 23P5020 to the drive (Link, press the switch, Ok) and wire its button pads to the PhotoMOS header. Either way the reed sensor stays. Because HF top is the untested generation, order the spare together with the spike parts (D10) so a no-go costs no extra week.

  3. Stage 2 · Device build and soak (two or three weekends, about €15 more)

    Firmware state machine from the diagram above, configurable delay (default 5 min), reed input with debounce, buzzer and LED warning, cancel button, rate limiting to respect the drive's duty rating, boot-quiet outputs, watchdog, optional MQTT or Home Assistant exposure. Bench test against the real drive, install, and run a one-week logged soak.

    Gate G2: 20 consecutive correct auto-closes, zero unintended openings, zero phantom presses across ten power cycles, one deliberate obstruction stopped by Blum's collision detection and handled by the retry/fault path.

  4. Stage 3 · OpenSCAD enclosure (one weekend, about €5)

    YAPP_Box project box: standoffs for the XIAO, slot for the nRF24 module with its antenna end clear of walls and metal, USB-C cutout, LED light tube, button cap, reed cable strain relief, M2 heat-set inserts (3.2 mm holes), VHB tape or two screws into the carcass. Fit-check with the NopSCADlib XIAO model, print in PETG, keep 30 mm from the Blum power supply.

    Gate G3: case closes without pressure on the antenna, reed cable is captive, button and LED usable, device re-passes G2 inside the case.

Safety and robustness

  • Blum stays in charge of the motion. Collision detection remains active; the device never holds a button and never sends more than one close per minute, within the drive's 10 moves per 10 minutes rating.
  • Warn before closing. 5 s of beeping and a blinking LED before any unattended close, the pattern required of unattended garage-door closers. A press on the button postpones by two minutes. An optional presence sensor postpones automatically.
  • Never close blind. A close is sent only while the reed reports "open". The radio path sends an absolute close, so a stale state cannot open the flap. The fallback toggle is gated by the same reed check.
  • Confirm, retry once, then stop. No "closed" within 8 s: one retry, then a latched fault with a blinking LED and no further action until a person closes the flap. This is also what happens when Blum's collision detection stops the flap on an obstacle.
  • Power-up discipline. Outputs on boot-quiet pins with pull-downs, no action in the first 60 s after power-up, reed read before any decision. After a mains cut the drive itself needs a switch press, which the device must not fake.
  • Master off. Blum already recommends a switched outlet for its power supply; the device dies with it.
  • Children and pets. Pinch area is the lever; the warning, the cancel button and the presence option cover it. A night schedule or longer delay is a cheap addition.
